Our Packaging Solutions

Packaging is selected to protect fat-soluble vitamin A from light, oxygen, and heat exposure, helping maintain stability throughout its shelf life. For vitamin A products, stability planning is especially important when formulas use retinoid forms such as retinol or retinal, where oxidation can reduce potency and affect label accuracy.

Blisters and Jars

Used For

  • Capsules
  • Tablets

Features

  • Blisters support clean single-dose use and reliable barrier protection.
  • Jars fit daily routines and bulk formats for regular consumption.
  • Barrier packs help protect vitamin A retinol ingredients from air and moisture.
  • Helps keep vitamin A stable as a fat soluble vitamin through shelf life.
  • Easy to label for compliance and ongoing support documentation.

Drops (Oil format)

Used For

  • Oil-based liquid supplements
  • Enhanced support formulas and skin-focused routines

Features

  • Dropper enables accurate daily intake and controlled dosing.
  • An oil carrier supports vitamin A stability because it is fat-soluble.
  • Packaging limits oxygen exposure and reduces oxidation from light.
  • Fits combinations with vitamin E, carotenoids, and other antioxidants.
  • Strong option for premium vitamin A supplements built for long-term maintenance.

Sachets and Doypacks

Used For

  • Energy powders
  • Portion-controlled servings
  • Bulk supplement formats
  • Drink mixes and wellness beverages

Features

  • Sachets provide clean single servings and simple logistics for distributors.
  • Doypacks are resealable and convenient for daily routines.
  • Better protection against oxidation and unwanted chemical change in storage.
  • Works well with blends and matrices used in functional concepts.
  • Great for fast production and export to many countries.

FAQ

What is vitamin A and what are its important functions?

Vitamin A is an essential vitamin with important functions in vision and tissue maintenance. It supports epithelial tissues and skin, and it contributes to normal immune system function and immune response.

What is vitamin A retinol, and how is it different from carotenoids?

Vitamin A retinol usually refers to retinoid forms such as retinol and retinal. Carotenoids such as β carotene can be used as an alternative approach depending on target audience, positioning, and the desired range of product options.

How does vitamin A support normal vision and night vision?

Vitamin A plays an important role in the visual cycle, which is why education content may mention night vision, retina, and visual maintenance. Our labeling approach keeps messaging in the “support normal function” lane, without disease claims.

What is vitamin A deficiency, and why do brands reference it carefully?

Deficiency can be linked to diet gaps and may affect vision and immune response. Some educational materials mention broader contexts such as liver status or anemia, but for supplements we keep claims conservative and compliant.

Is vitamin A fat soluble, and why does that matter for stability?

Yes, vitamin A is fat soluble, which affects storage and packaging decisions. Protection from light and oxygen is essential to reduce oxidative change and keep high stability through shelf life.
